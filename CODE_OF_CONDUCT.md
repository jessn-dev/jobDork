# Code of conduct

jobdork is made for people looking for work, in every field and every
country, and many of them are having a hard time. Everyone who takes part
here, in issues, pull requests, discussions and reviews, is expected to
treat others the way that deserves.

## What is expected

- **Be kind and assume good faith.** People report problems because they
  want the tool to work. A question that seems basic is still a fair one.
- **Be specific and respectful about work, not people.** Say what is wrong
  with the code or the idea, and why; never what is wrong with the person.
- **Make room for people who are new**, to programming, to open source, or
  to English. Explain, link, and be patient.
- **Accept that a decision may go another way**, and say so plainly when
  you disagree.
- **Protect privacy.** Never post someone else's resume, contact details,
  messages or keys, and do not ask for them. Your own are better kept out
  of public issues too.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, threats, or personal attacks, in public or private.
- Comments that demean people for who they are: their race, ethnicity,
  nationality, religion, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual
  orientation, age, disability, appearance, or how much money or experience
  they have.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their permission.
- Spam, advertising, or trolling, and repeatedly pushing a point after
  being asked to stop.
- Anything else a reasonable person would see as out of place in a project
  people come to for help.

## Where this applies

Everywhere the project is: this repository's issues, pull requests,
discussions and reviews, and anywhere someone speaks on the project's
behalf.

## Reporting a problem

Email **jessengolab.dev@gmail.com** with what happened, where (a link if
there is one), and who was involved. Reports are read by the maintainer
and kept private: the name of the person reporting is not shared without
their permission. You will get an answer, and if a report is about the
maintainer, say so, and it is handled with that in mind.

A security problem goes through [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) instead.

## What happens

The maintainer decides what to do, fairly and in proportion, after hearing
from the people involved where that is safe:

1. **A private word**: what was not acceptable, and what is expected.
2. **A warning**, with a time to keep away from the people involved.
3. **A temporary ban** from the project's spaces.
4. **A permanent ban**, for a pattern of harm or one serious act.

Comments, commits, issues and other contributions that break this code may
be edited, hidden or removed.

## Where this comes from

This code follows the ideas of the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/), version 2.1,
written in jobdork's own words.
