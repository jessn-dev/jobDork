Send feedback

Tell the people who make jobdork about a problem, an idea or a question. It becomes an issue on GitHub. You see exactly what is sent before you send it. Your resume, job posts and settings are never included. GitHub issues are public. Keys, tokens, email addresses and your home folder are taken out of the logs.

AI reader

A model reads the full advert against your resume and gives its own verdict beside the match score, and can read posting pages that do not say plainly whether the job is still open. It never hides a job post. Local models cost nothing; the others spend tokens on your key.

Readers are asked in order: the first does the work, and when it cannot answer (no key held, not reachable, rate limited, an error) the next one does, so a second reader keeps things going when the first is down. The same provider can be listed again with another model. A key is per provider, never written to a file, the config, the database or a log, nor sent back to this page: held in memory and forgotten when you stop jobdork, when you click Forget, or a minute after the last dashboard tab closes, or kept in this computer's keychain if you chose that under Settings, Your keys.

Judging needs a resume and at least 200 characters of advert. A 26B local model takes about a minute per job post, and the check adds two more calls to each.
A free key allows so many requests a minute and a day, set per model by the provider. jobdork keeps under them so the key does not run out: it spaces its requests, and stops scans' AI reading a few requests short of the day's limit, keeping those for what you ask yourself; the next reader in your list (Ollama, say) answers instead. Groq tells jobdork its numbers with every answer; Gemini only when it refuses, so jobdork learns them then. To stop sooner, type the daily limit Google AI Studio or the Groq console shows you. Gemini's day starts at midnight Pacific time.
Off unless you choose one. A checker runs the claim checks on everything a reader judges, writes and suggests, and scores each judged job post itself: scores 20 or more points apart are marked models disagree. Checkers are tried in order, like readers, so one that cannot answer does not leave an output unchecked. With none chosen or none ready, the claim checks go to another reader than the one that wrote it, and to the same one only when no other is ready. It never hides or blocks anything. About three more calls per output on its key; a local Ollama model costs nothing.

What you are looking for

Lists save as soon as you add or remove a row. Location, work arrangement and pay save with Save changes at the bottom. Each change applies itself to your job posts: they are re-screened, and posts that no longer fit, which you have not acted on, move to Dropped by your filters. Adding a job title, a country or an industry also starts a scan, to find the posts it brings.

Your certifications and licenses

An opened job post checks the ones its advert asks for against these and your resume. Write them as adverts do: RN, BLS, CPA, CDL, CISSP.

Countries to keep job posts from

Separate from the states and cities under Location, which are only about where you would go to an office. List every country you would work in or for: living in Baguio and open to remote work for US employers is the Philippines and the United States. A job post from anywhere else is dropped, and these also choose which sources search (USAJOBS only runs for the United States). None listed keeps job posts from every country.

Dealbreakers

Words that rule a job post out, read in the advert, not the title. Type them the way you would say them: security clearance, us citizen, client-site travel. Capitals, hyphens, dots and plurals do not matter, the usual other wordings are included ("security clearance" also finds "TS/SCI" and "polygraph"), and "no clearance required" does not count. One that hides the job post drops it; one that costs points keeps it, 8 points lower, with a warning.

Advanced: match with a regular expression

For anything plain words cannot say. A pattern is a Python regular expression, matched anywhere in the advert with capitals ignored, and taken as written: a "no" before a match does not cancel it.

(?:a|b)A group, usually to put an "or" inside wordsunpaid (?:trial|project)"unpaid trial", "unpaid project""unpaid leave"

To match one of . ? | ( ) [ ] { } + * ^ $ \ itself, put \ before it: C\+\+ finds "C++". More: Python's Regular Expression HOWTO, the full syntax reference, and regex101 (pick the Python flavour).

Resume

Used to rank job posts by skill overlap, offline and free. It is also what every drafted document is built from. Without one, a CV would be inventing a career rather than tailoring one.

Running in a container: the resume and the documents below are kept in a temporary folder, emptied when jobdork stops or starts. Upload the resume again after a restart, and download a letter you want to keep.

Projects

Work your resume does not describe well, in your own words: any kind of project, not only software. An app, articles you wrote, a campaign you ran, research, an event, volunteering, a personal project. Every project here goes into a tailored resume, the most relevant to the job first, even where the resume only lists it or links to it; its checks count them as part of your record. Fit scores count their skills too: job posts are re-scored when you save one. Where the section goes is decided per job post: near the top for a student or a career changer, beside the jobs for a freelancer, after them for someone with years in the field.

Resume review

The model on the AI page reads your resume and lists what to fix, each point quoting the line it is about. A point that quotes something not in your resume is dropped. To compare it with one job post, use Review resume for this post in that post's window.

Employer boards

These give full advert text, which aggregators do not. A board is added from the employer's own website, which is where its token is read from: tokens are rarely the company name, so they are never guessed.

Built-in employer boards

Employers' own boards that come with jobdork, across industries, each found on the employer's website and checked weekly for jobs. A scan reads the ones hiring in your countries; choose industries to read fewer, or switch single boards off. Reading more boards makes scans longer.
